The November 5 appointment of actor Park Bo-gum as global ambassador drew favorable reaction concentrated in Korean and Southeast Asian fandom, while the Mariah Carey holiday campaign ran into an organic backlash in North America branded \"cold and tone-deaf,\" and Europe carried a different kind of pressure tied to earnings and store restructuring rather than creative rejection.\n\n## 1. Today's Verdict — Ambassador News Lifted the Mood, the Holiday Campaign Cut It Down in North America\n\nThe conclusion first: Gucci's mood today split in opposite directions by region, and the fault line was the November 5 Park Bo-gum appointment on one side and the North American backlash against the Mariah Carey campaign on the other. Northeast Asia (NEA) was the most positive axis, as the Park Bo-gum selection spread quickly through Korean entertainment and fashion outlets and fan accounts[1][2], and Southeast Asia (SEA) rode the same wave as Thai and Philippine K-ent press picked it up in a celebratory tone[3][4]. North America (NA), by contrast, saw organized criticism of the holiday campaign weigh on the mood — a TikTok series that passed 1.3 million views framed the campaign as \"flaunting cruelty\" amid economic hardship[5], and Yahoo Entertainment also rounded up fan reactions under a \"depressing\" frame[15]. Europe's (EU) burden, however, is of a different character — not a creative rejection but structural pressure from Q3 Gucci retail sales declines and lease uncertainty at the Avenue Montaigne store weighing on sentiment[10][12]. In short, North America faced a genuine organic creative backlash while Europe faced business-fundamentals pressure — the same surface phenomenon did not repeat identically across the two markets.\n\n![Report on Park Bo-gum's selection as Gucci global ambassador](https://images.khan.co.kr/article/2026/11/05/news-p.v1.20261105.795d4c4249944ac1bc00436a4296f1e2_P1.jpg \"Khan/Sports Kyunghyang|https://sports.khan.co.kr/article/202611051732003/\")\n\n## 2. Evidence and Counter-Evidence — The Narrow Reach of the Fandom Win, and the Unresolved Sales-and-Store Narrative\n\nIt must first be acknowledged that while the Park Bo-gum news is a genuine positive, its reach is confined largely to fandom. Korean communities praised the \"bad boy\" transformation in the W Korea editorial[7], and the buzz spread even to Japanese fan blogs[8], but this is a reaction to brand-driven activation, not opinion verified by purchase behavior. Underneath sits a heavier narrative — Gucci's Q3 comparable sales fell 6%, dragging on the group[9][10], and Kering raised its target to at least 125 net store closures for the year, roughly half of them Gucci stores[11]. In Europe, this was compounded by reports of lease uncertainty at the Avenue Montaigne store in Paris[6][12]. There is a counter-argument, however. The October 30 opening of the new Avenue Montaigne flagship resolved that uncertainty on a positive note[13], and a Bernstein analyst scored Demna's debut 7.6 out of 10, assessing that \"the direction is right\"[14]. That said, the fact that shoes and bags were flagged as weak points overlaps with what could be an Achilles' heel for the holiday product line.\n\n![Community praise for Park Bo-gum's bad boy transformation in the Gucci editorial](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhMOnvPQwbtvKYM4Reytvnc3sGufn1nMhMOlozl1rTpp3HRoVGWs7LRkoj7pZrzQpAG9fUiDVKdHnt82JlOFydrw3_ZFQueYFGB3lUOuhe5b6XxbZ4SBZIG7AC4XM5aWaV6ZUvoUOU__sTH978_AAF4nghyGkwAU2z64b_WLwijFDNTVJV5PE17ApEF4gZl/w1200-h630-p-k-no-nu/image.png \"theqoo (via pannchoa)|https://www.pannchoa.com/2026/10/theqoo-current-whereabouts-of-park.html\")\n\n## 3. Yahoo Entertainment in North America has already rounded up fan reactions under a \"depressing\" frame[15], and if that tone migrates into consumer-review-style YouTube content (\"Is Gucci good again?\"), the backlash could be elevated from a view-count issue to a brand-reputation issue[16]. Recommendations — This Week's Actions\n\nOne, adjust the emphasis of the Mariah Carey campaign's messaging rather than overhauling the creative — prioritize distributing a secondary cut for North America that shifts the center of gravity from luxury-and-display toward \"warmth and gift-giving,\" to preemptively address the ongoing organic backlash. Two, pull Park Bo-gum out of fandom buzz and into a product narrative — explicitly tie ambassador content to the mid-November Butterfly store launch and the Japan-exclusive leather goods launch so the window to convert goodwill into traffic is not missed. Three, get ahead of the store-closure and sales-decline narrative with a proactive frame — make the Avenue Montaigne flagship's \"density and experience\" narrative the official message, so closures read as restructuring rather than retreat.
