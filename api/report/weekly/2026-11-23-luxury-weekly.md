세컨더리 시장에서도 하드럭셔리의 열기가 확인됐다 — 필립스 경매 사상 첫 1억 달러 돌파와 카르티에 탱크의 라이스트 복귀가 그 신호다[21][22]. 즉, 지갑이 열리는 영역이 핸드백에서 보석·시계 쪽으로 이동하고 있다는 뜻이다.\n\n![필립스 제네바 워치 경매 사상 첫 1억 달러 변화 — 중국 상단 붕괴, 미국·일본·2선 상권의 잔열\n\n권역 구조는 세 방향으로 정리된다 — 중국은 꼭대기까지 식었고, 미국·일본에는 아직 온기가 남아 있으며, 미국 2선 상권으로 수요가 확산 중이다.\n\n먼저 중국은 '마지막 안전지대'까지 흔들렸다 — 에르메스·카르티에 본토 매출이 8~9월 두 자릿수 감소하자, 중화권 안에서도 수요의 지리적 재배치가 진행됐다[1]. 본토 감소분 일부를 홍콩·마카오가 상쇄하고 있고, 루이비통은 마카오에서 카지노 운영사 샌즈차이나와 손잡고 브랜드 최초의 마카오 쇼를 열어 본토 트래픽이 빠진 자리를 관광·중화권 주변부 수요로 메우려는 움직임을 가시화했다[1][32]. 반면 미국·일본에는 잔열이 남아, LVMH 미국 5%·일본 6% 성장과 에르메스 미국·유럽 성장이 이를 뒷받침한다[9][8]. 더불어 미국 2선 상권으로의 확산도 이어져, 샤넬·에르메스·로로피아나가 샌디에이고 웨스트필드 UTC에 동반 첫 매장을 열었고 카르티에는 베벌리힐스에 네 번째 '템플'급 플래그십을 2027년 개장 목표로 추진 중이다[33][34]. 정리하자면, '중국은 꼭대기까지 식었지만 미국·일본·2선 상권은 아직 온기가 남아 있다'가 이번 주 지역 지형의 정확한 묘사다.\n\n\n## 6. 트렌드 변곡 — 승계의 두 속도, 'AI 크래프트워싱', 가격 중앙집권화\n\n이번 주 트렌드 변곡은 세 축이다. 첫째, 승계의 두 속도다 — 켈링이 발렌시아가 후임을 2주 만에 확정하면서 생로랑 공석을 7주 넘게 비워둔 것은, 그룹이 어느 하우스를 '지킬 자산'으로 보는지 드러내는 선행 지표다[10][11]. 승계 속도 자체가 자원 집중의 방향을 말해주며, 생로랑의 긴 공백은 그룹 최대 매출원임에도 후임 리스크가 그만큼 크다는 역설적 신호다.\n\n둘째, 'AI 크래프트워싱'이라는 새 리스크다 — 디올이 '전부 손으로 그렸다'던 홀리데이 애니메이션에 중간 프레임 자동 보간을 썼다고 뒤늦게 인정하면서 논란이 일주일 넘게 이어졌고, 전 디즈니 애니메이터가 전 프레임을 손으로 그린 프라다 캠페인과 대조되며 비판이 커졌다[5][6][35]. 공예·헤리티지를 마케팅 중심에 둔 하우스일수록 제작 과정의 진실성이 드러날 때 받는 타격이 더 크다[6]. 다만 균형추도 있다 — 프라다는 손그림을 차별화 포인트로 삼아 같은 리스크를 자산으로 바꿨고, 문화 매거진 '프라다 인덱스'를 창간해 제품 밖 권위를 쌓는 쪽으로 움직였다[35][36]. 같은 주 AI는 세 얼굴로 나타났다 — 마케팅 신뢰 리스크(디올), 콘텐츠 전략 자산(LVMH 미디어 오픈AI 제휴), 거시 밸류에이션 압박(기술주 매도 전이)이다[5][37][9].\n\n![프라다 손그림 홀리데이 캠페인](https://media.gqmiddleeast.com/photos/6aec806cd9d686c4355d3421/16:9/w_1280,c_limit/Prada%20Holiday%202026%20Adv%20campaign%20(4).jpg \"GQ Middle East|https://www.gqmiddleeast.com/article/prada-holiday-collection-2026\")\n\n셋째, 가격·유통 통제의 중앙집권화다 — 켈링이 구찌 가격 인하 직후 그룹 가격정책 총괄을 신설하고 2026년 순폐점 목표를 125개 이상으로 상향하면서 구찌를 하우스별 폐점 최다 브랜드로 재확인했다[14][38]. 이는 희소성·할당 관행을 둘러싼 법적 압력과도 맞물린다 — 에르메스는 버킨 끼워팔기 반독점 소송을 9항소법원에서 방어 중이다[39].\n\n## 7. 떠오르는 플레이어 — 셀린·프라다 뷰티·로에베·보테가\n\n이번 주 모멘텀이 숫자로 받쳐지는 떠오르는 플레이어는 네 곳이다. 먼저 **셀린**은 리복 협업 스니커즈 '프리스타일 로'가 즉시 매진되며 라이스트 인덱스 10위로 5계단 상승했고, 남성 수요가 전년 대비 62% 급증했으며 마이클 라이더가 WWD 올해의 남성복 디자이너에 올랐다[40]. **프라다 뷰티**는 1~9월 로열티 매출이 77% 급증하며 로레알 럭스 라이선스 포트폴리오 중 최고 성장을 기록했고, '파라디그므' 향수가 틱톡에서 개별 영상 4만5천 좋아요를 넘기며 2차 확산을 이어갔다[41]. **로에베**는 맥컬러·에르난데스 체제 1년 만에 LVMH 실적 발표에서 '첫 모멘텀 조짐' 브랜드로 언급됐고[42], **보테가 베네타**는 켈링 공시에서 전 지역 순차 가속을 보인 유일한 하우스로 꼽혔다[43].\n\n![프라다 뷰티·아이웨어 로열티 +77%, 파라디그므 틱톡 확산](https://wwd.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/miu-miu-spring-27-rtw-pfw-r-gg-007.jpeg?w=1200 \"WWD|https://wwd.com/business-news/financial/prada-group-spend-more-dreams-strong-nine-month-results-1239380762/\")\n\n다만 공통된 균형추가 필요하다 — 모두 '관심·언급·로열티' 지표이거나 '첫 조짐' 수준이지, 지속 매출·마진 전환이 확정된 것은 아니다. 셀린의 라이스트 상승은 검색·소셜 관심도이며, 프라다 뷰티의 로열티 호조는 라이선스 수익이지 프라다 그룹 본업 리테일(9개월 +2%)의 건전성을 보장하지 않는다[40][41]. 이 상승 신호들을 꿰는 공통 문양은, 스니커즈·향수·협업 같은 '진입 가격대 액세서리'가 브랜드 체온을 끌어올리는 1차 엔진이 됐다는 것이다 — 다만 미우미우의 3% 역성장이 그 반증으로, 바이럴은 매출 전환 장치 없이는 영구 수요로 굳지 않는다[31].\n\n## 8. 구찌 함의 — 위협, 기회, 대응 각도\n\n구찌 입장에서 이번 주 데이터의 결론은, 위협과 기회가 같은 뿌리에서 나온다는 것이다 — 중국 둔화가 에르메스·카르티에까지 번지면서 '구찌만의 문제'라는 비판은 약해졌지만, 동시에 켈링 주얼리가 11%로 홀로 성장하면서 포트폴리오 안에서 구찌의 역성장이 더 도드라졌다[1][7]. 거시 역풍은 변명이 되어주지만, 그룹 내부 비교에서는 오히려 더 불리한 대조가 성립한다.\n\n**위협**. 첫째, 하드럭셔리로의 성장 이동은 구찌가 속한 패션·가죽을 상대적 약자로 밀어내고 있고, 켈링 주얼리가 그룹 성장 서사를 가져가면서 구찌 회복 지연이 길어질수록 그룹의 관심·자원이 주얼리로 옮겨갈 위험이 있다[7]. 둘째, 가격 중앙집권화는 구찌의 가격·매장 자율성이 그룹 승인 체계로 넘어간다는 뜻이며, 맥퀸의 54명 감원은 회복이 정체될 경우 같은 논리가 구찌 인력·매장에도 적용될 수 있는 선례다[12][14]. 셋째, 디올형 '크래프트워싱' 리스크는 장인정신을 메시지로 쓰는 구찌에 그대로 노출돼 있다[6].\n\n![알렉산더 맥퀸 54명 Drapers|https://theimpression.com/alexander-mcqueen-moves-forward-with-layoffs-as-restructuring-intensifies/\")\n\n**기회**. 첫째, 셀린·로에베 사례는 교체 체제가 1년 안에 모멘텀 신호를 낼 수 있음을 보여주며, 이는 뎀나·벨레티니 체제에도 열려 있는 경로다[40][42]. 둘째, 버버리가 다니엘 리 체제에서 상반기 6%·흑자 전환으로 턴어라운드를 숫자로 증명한 것은 디자이너 교체형 회복의 소요 기간에 대한 참고점이다[27]. 셋째, 상하이 크루즈 2028 쇼(2027년 5월, 중국 진출 30주년)는 중국 승부수가 될 수 있다 — 다만 디올이 사흘 앞서 같은 도시에서 크루즈 쇼를 여는 만큼 차별화 서사가 필수다[44].\n\n**대응 각도**. (1) 하드럭셔리 전환에 맞춰 자사 액세서리·주얼리 포트폴리오의 그룹 내 입지를 재점검하되, 세컨더리 열기는 신제품 1차 수요와 다른 지표임을 분리해 평가한다[22]. (2) 상하이 쇼는 디올과의 사흘 차 동일 도시 개최를 전제로 차별화 서사를 선제 설계한다[44]. (3) 홀리데이 캠페인의 'AI 투명성'을 선제 공개해 크래프트워싱 리스크를 사전 차단한다[6]. (4) 머라이어 캐리 '구찌 기프트' 캠페인의 3주 지속 노출은 활성화 성과이지 수요 신호가 아니며, 앞서 기록된 '차갑다'는 소비자 반응과 분리해 평가한다[45]. 본 분석의 판단은, 구찌의 회복 시계를 섹터 평균과 분리해 보되, 다음 확인 지점은 골드만삭스가 -4.3%로 낮춘 4분기 중국 매출이 그 전망대로 굳는지 여부라는 데 있다[7][13].\n\n## 9. Executive Summary (English)\n\nThe clearest structural shift this week is that China's slowdown reached the very top of the pyramid, moving luxury's growth engine visibly from fashion-and-leather toward hard luxury: Hermès and Cartier both saw double-digit mainland-China declines in Aug-Sep[1], Hermès growth decelerated to 4.3% and its stock hit a four-year low[2], while Kering Jewelry grew 11% as the group's lone double-digit engine against Gucci's -6%[7]. That said, hard luxury is not a safe harbor either — Richemont shares fell up to 9% on mere deceleration despite 18% half-year jewelry growth, and LVMH Watches & Jewelry growth halved to 6%[3][4]; the real signal is that markets now punish any deceleration equally harshly. A newly salient risk is \"craftwashing\" — Dior's week-long AI-concealment controversy over a supposedly hand-drawn holiday film, sharpened by contrast with Prada's genuinely hand-drawn campaign, shows craft-first houses face amplified trust damage when production transparency fails[5][6][35]; AI appeared simultaneously as a content asset (LVMH-OpenAI) and a macro valuation drag (tech sell-off spilling into luxury stocks)[9][37]. Creative succession ran at two speeds: Kering named Balenciaga's new CD in two weeks while top-revenue Saint Laurent stayed vacant past seven, and McQueen opened redundancies for a third of its Italian workforce[10][11][12]. Four rising players gained evidenced momentum — Celine (Reebok collab, men's demand +62%, Lyst top 10), Prada Beauty (royalty revenue +77%, Paradigme TikTok virality), Loewe (first momentum in LVMH commentary) and Bottega Veneta (sole accelerating Kering house) — though all are interest/royalty/early-sign metrics, with Miu Miu's swing to -3% as the cautionary counter-case[40][41][42][43][31]. Groups also moved to centralize pricing and distribution away from houses, Kering hiring a group pricing director right after Gucci's 30% cut and reconfirming 125+ closures led by Gucci[14][38]. For Gucci, threat and opportunity share one root: the macro headwind weakens the \"Gucci-only problem\" critique, yet the intra-portfolio contrast against +11% jewelry grows starker[1][7]. The next checkpoint is whether Q4 China sales settle at Goldman's downgraded -4.3%[13].\n\n![로에베 '첫 모멘텀 조짐' LVMH 3분기 아니다.\n- 리세일 지표(ChronoPulse·리세일가): 세컨더리(중고·경매) 시장의 검색·거래 활성도와 거래가. 신제품 1차 판매와는 다른 지표로, 수집가 수요의 대리변수(proxy)다.\n- 로열티 매출(royalty revenue): 라이선스 사업에서 파트너사로부터 받는 수수료 수입. 브랜드 본업 리테일 매출과 별개 항목이다.\n- 크래프트워싱(craftwashing): 실제 제작 과정이 수공예·장인정신 주장과 다를 때 발생하는 신뢰 훼손 리스크. 트렌드 분석이 이번 주 명명한 구조적 리스크 범주다.\n- 활성화(activation) 보도: 브랜드가 집행한 캠페인·행사·앰배서더 소식 등 자체 마케팅 활동 보도. 소비자 여론·수요 개선의 증거가 아니다.\n- 메티에다르(Métiers d'Art): 샤넬이 매년 다른 도시에서 공방 수작업을 조명하며 선보이는 컬렉션 시리즈.\n- 지수 관련 수치는 같은 날짜의 지수 해석 리포트 참조.\n\n## 11. 출처\n\n[1] Bloomberg — 에르메스·카르티에 중국 본토 8~9월 두 자릿수 감소 — Week in Review — the Slowdown Climbed to the Top of the Pyramid\n\nThis week's dominant structure converges on one point — even Hermes and Cartier, long called the \"last safe zone\" amid China's slowdown, saw their mainland sales crack[1]. In data cited by Bloomberg, both brands' mainland China sales fell by double digits in August-September; because these two houses were considered the industry's most reliable shoppers remaining, the slowdown signals not a single brand's strategic failure but a structural retreat in Chinese demand itself[1]. Hermes's Q3 growth decelerated from 6.7% in Q2 to 4.3%; leather goods grew nearly 11% but watches contracted, and China grew just 1.9%[2][8]. In the week following the results, the share price fell a further 5.3%, dropping to a four-year low[2].\n\n![Hermes retains sector lead but Q3 deceleration](https://myquotabag.com/assets/brief-images/2026-11-november.jpg \"Vogue Business / MyQuotaBag|https://myquotabag.com/brief/2026-11-november/\")\n\nThat said, this structural reading needs its own counterweight — the slowdown is not simultaneous across every region. LVMH grew 5% in the US and 6% in Japan in Q3, and Hermes leather goods grew 8.8% in the US and 7.2% in Europe[9][8]. Yet capital markets' scrutiny has been harsh regardless: LVMH shares fell about 5% on the week as the AI-driven tech-stock selloff spilled into luxury, sending the stock to the bottom of the CAC 40 and more than 41% below its start-of-year level[9]. In short, this week's story is not about which individual house won or lost, but a sector-wide structural shift in which markets have begun re-rating both the cooling top tier and the still-warm second tier more strictly at the same time.\n\n## 3. Key Moves by Brand Group\n\nThis week's group-level moves converge on one sentence — even within the same group, the prescription and its pace diverged house by house.\n\nWithin Kering, the asymmetry of succession and restructuring surfaced simultaneously — Balenciaga confirmed Martine Rose as its first female creative director in its 109-year history just two weeks after Piccioli's exit, a clear signal of a return to the sharper aesthetic of the Demna era[10], while Saint Laurent, the group's highest-revenue house, went more than seven weeks without naming Vaccarello's successor[11]. Alexander McQueen, still without a creative director, has begun layoff procedures affecting 54 of its roughly 164 Italian factory staff, about a third of the workforce[12]. Meanwhile jewelry (Boucheron, Pomellato) grew 11% in Q3, remaining the group's sole double-digit growth engine, but the market still fixes its share-price attention on the Gucci recovery narrative, so other houses' growth has not been absorbed as an offsetting story[7][13]. Kering's hire of former Nike executive Francisco Ochando as a newly created group-wide pricing director — coming right after Gucci's 30% price cut on the Jackie bag — signals that house-level pricing autonomy is shifting to central group control[14].\n\n![Saint Laurent creative-director seat vacant for seven weeks](https://imgix.bustle.com/uploads/image/2026/10/9/92363f06/0225collectionsselectionsaintlaurentlo_4044133-revoked.jpg?w=1200&h=630&fit=crop&crop=focalpoint&fm=jpg&fp-x=0.3978&fp-y=0.2898 \"W Magazine / Style Rave|https://www.wmagazine.com/fashion/anthony-vaccarello-leaving-saint-laurent\")\n\nAt LVMH, a \"model case\" and a \"crisis case\" of director handovers emerged in the same week — Fendi CEO Anna Safroncik's (Angeloglou's) promotion to Dior Couture CEO and the hiring of Delphine Bellini, who revived Schiaparelli, to lead Louis Vuitton's Champs-Elysees flagship both show the internal succession pipeline functioning[15][16], while Dior's holiday film drew over a week of trust controversy over the gap between its \"entirely hand-drawn\" claim and the director's belated admission of AI interpolation use[5][6]. Further, LVMH's complete withdrawal from the bidding for a stake in Armani, clearing the way for L'Oreal and EssilorLuxottica, shows that the center of gravity in independent-house acquisition competition is shifting from fashion parent groups to beauty licensors[17].\n\nAt Hermes-Chanel, the two houses' trajectories diverged — Hermes showed that even the sector's No. 1 house is not exempt, as its slowdown fed directly into its share price[2], while Chanel, under the Blazy regime, saw secondhand prices trade up to 15% above retail, a signal of excess demand, and previewed its Metiers d'Art show in Rome on December 2, extending its heritage buzz[18][19]. Category Temperature — Hard Luxury Cools Less, Leather Cools More\n\nThe conclusion on category temperature is that the gap between fashion-and-leather and hard luxury widened most visibly this week — Kering jewelry grew 11% in Q3, the group's sole double-digit growth engine, with in-store sales up 18%, while Gucci, in the same group, contracted 6%[7]. The secondary market also confirmed hard luxury's heat — the Phillips auction's first-ever breach of $100 million and the Cartier Tank's return to the Lyst Index are the signals[21][22]. Second, resale and auction data are secondary-market indicators, not primary new-product sales — even the Cartier Tank trades 27.4% below retail, and the Phillips record is concentrated in vintage and independent watchmakers[22][21]. Ready-to-wear and accessories, by contrast, sit at the coldest point, and Miu Miu's swing to a 3% Q3 decline after years of hypergrowth is a counter-case showing that viral momentum does not harden into permanent demand without a mechanism to convert it into sales[31].\n\n## 5. Regional Shifts — China's Top Tier Cracks, Residual Warmth in the US, Japan, and Secondary Markets\n\nRegional structure sorts into three directions — China has cooled all the way to the top, the US and Japan retain residual warmth, and demand is spreading into America's secondary shopping districts.\n\nChina, first, saw even its \"last safe zone\" shaken — as Hermes and Cartier mainland sales fell by double digits in August-September, a geographic reshuffling of demand is underway even within the Greater China sphere[1]. Hong Kong and Macau are offsetting part of the mainland decline, and Louis Vuitton's first-ever Macau show, staged in partnership with casino operator Sands China, makes visible the move to fill the gap left by mainland traffic with tourism and peripheral Greater China demand[1][32]. The US and Japan, by contrast, retain residual warmth, supported by LVMH's 5% US and 6% Japan growth and Hermes's US and European growth[9][8]. Demand is also spreading into America's secondary shopping districts — Chanel, Hermes, and Loro Piana jointly opened their first stores at San Diego's Westfield UTC, and Cartier is pursuing a fourth \"temple\"-class flagship in Beverly Hills targeted for a 2027 opening[33][34]. In sum, the accurate description of this week's regional map is that China has cooled to the very top, while the US, Japan, and secondary American markets still retain warmth.\n\n\n## 6. Trend Inflection — Two Speeds of Succession, \"AI Craftwashing,\" and Pricing Centralization\n\nThis week's trend inflection runs along three axes. First, two speeds of succession — Kering confirmed Balenciaga's successor within two weeks while leaving Saint Laurent's seat vacant for more than seven, a leading indicator of which houses the group views as \"assets to protect\"[10][11]. The pace of succession itself signals the direction of resource concentration, and Saint Laurent's long vacancy is a paradoxical signal that successor risk is just as large despite the house being the group's top revenue source.\n\nSecond, a new risk called \"AI craftwashing\" — controversy over Dior's holiday animation ran for more than a week after the house belatedly admitted using automated frame interpolation despite having claimed it was \"entirely hand-drawn,\" with criticism sharpened by contrast with a Prada campaign in which a former Disney animator drew every frame by hand[5][6][35]. Houses that place craft and heritage at the center of their marketing suffer greater damage when the truth about their production process comes out[6]. That said, a counterweight exists — Prada turned the same risk into an asset by making hand-drawing a point of differentiation, and launched the cultural magazine \"Prada Index\" to build authority beyond the product itself[35][36]. AI showed three faces in the same week — a marketing trust risk (Dior), a content strategy asset (LVMH Media's OpenAI partnership), and a macro valuation pressure (tech-stock selloff spillover)[5][37][9].\n\n![Prada's hand-drawn holiday campaign](https://media.gqmiddleeast.com/photos/6aec806cd9d686c4355d3421/16:9/w_1280,c_limit/Prada%20Holiday%202026%20Adv%20campaign%20(4).jpg \"GQ Middle East|https://www.gqmiddleeast.com/article/prada-holiday-collection-2026\")\n\nThird, centralization of pricing and distribution control — right after Gucci's price cut, Kering created a new group-wide pricing director role and raised its 2026 net store-closure target to 125-plus, reconfirming Gucci as the house with the most closures[14][38]. Celine's Lyst rise reflects search and social interest, and Prada Beauty's royalty strength is license income, which does not guarantee the health of Prada Group's own core retail business (+2% over nine months)[40][41]. The common thread running through these rising signals is that \"entry-price accessories\" such as sneakers, fragrance, and collaborations have become the primary engine for raising a brand's temperature — though Miu Miu's 3% contraction is the counter-case, showing that virality does not harden into permanent demand without a conversion mechanism[31].\n\n## 8. Gucci Implications — Threats, Opportunities, Response Angles\n\nThe conclusion from Gucci's vantage point is that threat and opportunity share the same root this week — as China's slowdown spread to Hermes and Cartier, the \"Gucci-only problem\" critique weakened, but at the same time, Kering jewelry's standalone 11% growth made Gucci's own contraction stand out more sharply within the portfolio[1][7]. The macro headwind offers an excuse, but the intra-group comparison produces a more unfavorable contrast.\n\nThreats. First, the shift of growth toward hard luxury is pushing fashion-and-leather, the category Gucci belongs to, into relative weakness, and as Kering jewelry takes over the group's growth narrative, there is a risk that the group's attention and resources migrate toward jewelry the longer Gucci's recovery is delayed[7]. Second, pricing centralization means Gucci's pricing and store autonomy is shifting into group-level approval, and McQueen's 54-person layoff is a precedent showing the same logic could apply to Gucci's workforce and stores if its recovery stalls[12][14]. Third, the Dior-style \"craftwashing\" risk is directly relevant to Gucci, which also uses craftsmanship as a core message[6].\n\n![Alexander McQueen begins layoff procedures affecting 54 staff](https://theimpression.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/McQueen-Fall-2026.jpg \"The Impression / Drapers|https://theimpression.com/alexander-mcqueen-moves-forward-with-layoffs-as-restructuring-intensifies/\")\n\nOpportunities. First, the Celine and Loewe cases show that a creative-director handover can produce momentum signals within a year, a path also open to the Demna-Valletini regime at Gucci[40][42]. Second, Burberry's proof of a turnaround with numbers — 6% H1 growth and a return to profit under Daniel Lee — is a useful reference point for how long a designer-led recovery takes[27]. Third, the Shanghai Cruise 2028 show (May 2027, marking 30 years in China) could be a decisive China move — though because Dior is holding its own Cruise show in the same city just three days earlier, a clearly differentiated narrative is essential[44].\n\nResponse angles. (1) Reassess the group-wide positioning of Gucci's own accessories and jewelry portfolio in light of the hard-luxury shift, while keeping secondary-market heat analytically separate from primary new-product demand[22]. (2) Design the Shanghai show's differentiation narrative proactively, given that it will be held in the same city just three days after Dior's[44]. (3) Preemptively disclose \"AI transparency\" in holiday campaigns to head off craftwashing risk before it starts[6]. (4) Treat the three-week sustained exposure of the Mariah Carey \"Gucci Gift\" campaign as an activation-performance metric, not a demand signal, and assess it separately from the previously recorded \"cold\" consumer reaction[45]. Our read is that Gucci's recovery timeline should be assessed separately from the sector average, and the next checkpoint to watch is whether Q4 China sales settle at Goldman Sachs's downgraded -4.3% forecast[7][13].\n\n## 9. Notes\n\n- Comparable/organic sales: growth calculated on a like-for-like basis after stripping out currency and M&A effects. A better indicator of actual demand trends than the reported figure.\n- Lyst Index: a quarterly \"heat\" ranking of brands and products compiled by online shopping platform Lyst from search, view, and sales data. An interest-level indicator, not confirmed revenue or margin.\n- Resale indicators (ChronoPulse, resale prices): search and transaction activity and prices in the secondary (used/auction) market. Distinct from primary new-product sales; a proxy for collector demand.\n- Royalty revenue: fee income a brand receives from licensing partners. A separate line item from the brand's own core retail revenue.\n- Craftwashing: the trust-damage risk that arises when actual production processes diverge from claims of handcraft or artisanship. A structural risk category named by this week's trend analysis.\n- Activation coverage: press coverage of campaigns, events, and ambassador news that a brand itself executes as marketing. Not evidence of consumer sentiment or demand improvement.\n- Metiers d'Art: Chanel's annual collection series, staged in a different city each year, spotlighting hand-crafted atelier work.\n- Index-related figures should be cross-checked against the same-date index-interpretation report.\n\n## 10.
